04 / Bank reconciliation

Two different balances. One reconciled result.

The bank statement and the cash book differ for valid timing reasons and an unrecorded fee. Here both sides are reconciled to the same adjusted closing cash balance.

Bank statement to adjusted balance

Timing differences: The deposit is recorded in the cash book but not yet credited by the bank; the supplier payment is in the books but has not cleared.

Cash book to adjusted balance

Book adjustment: A$12,000 of bank charges must be posted using journal . Deposits in transit and outstanding payments need no duplicate journal.

08 / Accountant's findings

What needs attention after the numbers agree?

A reconciled balance does not remove collection, liquidity, cut-off or compliance risk. These findings are analytical conclusions drawn from the fictional case study, not claims about a real organisation.

Methodology & limitations

All opening balances, invoices, cash receipts, journal entries, bank timing items and reconciliations were designed as a self-contained illustrative teaching dataset. Each financial number on this page is derived from the embedded data and subject to mathematical cross-checks. The scenario does not model income tax, PAYG withholding, superannuation, inventory impairment, GST exceptions, bad debt provisions or a full statutory reporting framework. All accounting judgements and simulated source-document references are illustrative, not verified external evidence.

Australian GST reporting terminology was informed by the Australian Taxation Office. ATO activity statements guidance ↗
