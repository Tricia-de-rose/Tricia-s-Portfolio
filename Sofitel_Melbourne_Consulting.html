01 The engagement

Consulting for Sofitel Melbourne

Turning responsible sourcing principles into practical business recommendations.

I worked as a consultant for Sofitel Melbourne on Collins, a luxury hotel under the Accor brand, on a project examining ethical procurement, supplier oversight and the prevention of modern slavery in hospitality supply chains.

The engagement involved reviewing supplier governance, analysing procurement processes and applying strategic frameworks to identify improvement opportunities. I contributed to a proposed Supplier Excellence Programme focused on transparency, accountability and ongoing verification.

Our recommendations were designed to support responsible sourcing while considering commercial feasibility, resource constraints and the guest experience.

Why modern slavery matters in hospitality

Food production, textiles, cleaning, laundry and contracted services can involve multiple employers and production stages. Supplier screening and monitoring help organisations identify where additional human rights due diligence may be needed.

What is the difference between SDGs and legal compliance?

The SDGs are global development goals. Modern slavery laws and reporting obligations are separate legal frameworks. This case study presents a consulting strategy, not a legal opinion or an assessment of certified compliance.

05 Business insights

What the analysis highlighted

Four important lessons about building credible responsible sourcing systems.

Governance

Policies need clear ownership

Supplier standards are most effective when the responsible teams, approval processes and escalation routes are understood.

Evidence

Approval is not the same as verification

Operational checks and signed declarations may need supporting evidence to demonstrate responsible sourcing practices.

Traceability

Risk can sit beyond direct suppliers

Manufacturing, recruitment and subcontracting may take place several stages before a product or service reaches the hotel.

Feasibility

Prioritisation makes progress practical

A proportionate approach allows an organisation to focus more attention where labour related exposure and uncertainty may be greater.

06 Strategic recommendation

The Supplier Excellence Programme

A proposed programme designed to strengthen supplier governance through practical actions and progressive improvement.

PILLAR 01

Visibility and Transparency

Build a consistent picture of the supplier network and its supporting information.

  • Create a central supplier information register.
  • Record review status and documentation.
  • Encourage disclosure of relevant upstream relationships.
PILLAR 02

Governance and Accountability

Make expectations and responsibilities clear across the procurement process.

  • Assign supplier governance responsibilities.
  • Introduce responsible procurement standards.
  • Provide accessible training and guidance.
PILLAR 03

Verification and Improvement

Support more reliable assurance and ongoing supplier engagement.

  • Use proportionate document reviews.
  • Consider targeted external assessments.
  • Track issues and corrective actions.
